For decades, European procurement teams operated on a simple rule: **Chinese steel = lowest price. Indian steel = higher risk.** That rule no longer holds.

Since January 1, 2026, the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) has added a new variable to the equation: **verified emissions data**. The mill with the lowest base price is no longer the mill with the lowest total cost. The mill that can prove its carbon footprint is.

## Why Indian Scrap-Based Mills Win on Carbon

The Indian mill in our analysis uses a **scrap-based induction furnace route**. This is critical for three reasons:

**1.** **Scrap recycling eliminates primary ironmaking.** No blast furnace means no coke combustion, no sintering, and no direct reduction. The carbon intensity drops from ~2.0 tCO₂/tonne to under 0.1 tCO₂/tonne for the melting stage.

**2.** **Electricity is the only major emission source.** At 0.640 tCO₂/tonne, grid electricity accounts for 94% of total emissions. As India's grid decarbonizes — solar capacity is growing 15% annually — this number will fall further.

**3.** **The EU rewards low-carbon intensity.** With verified data, the 97.5% free allocation shield applies. A mill at 0.684 tCO₂/tonne pays effectively nothing in CBAM. A mill at 2.5 tCO₂/tonne pays full default rates.

Chinese mills are not blind to this. Baowu, Shagang, and Ansteel are investing heavily in hydrogen-based DRI and EAF capacity. But the transition will take 5–10 years. **Today, verified Indian scrap-based steel is structurally cheaper under CBAM than verified Chinese BF-BOF steel.**

## The Quality Question: Is Indian Steel Good Enough?

This is the objection we hear most often. European buyers associate Chinese mills with scale and consistency, and Indian mills with variability. The reality is more nuanced.

### Chinese Mills: Scale and Standardization

Chinese tier-1 mills (Baowu, Shagang, HBIS) produce to global standards. Their advantages:

**✓** Massive volume capacity — 10,000+ tonne orders are routine

**✓** Standardized grades — S235JR, S355J2, C45 produced in volume with tight tolerances

**✓** Established logistics — regular sailings from Shanghai, Tianjin, Qingdao to Rotterdam

The downside: **CBAM verification is still patchy.** Only a handful of Chinese mills have completed EU-recognized third-party audits. Most provide "self-declared" emissions data that customs may reject.

### Indian Mills: Niche and Improving

Indian tier-1 mills (Tata Steel, JSW, SAIL, and select long-product specialists) have invested heavily in quality systems over the past decade. Their advantages:

**✓** Lower carbon intensity — scrap-based EAF routes as low as 0.684 tCO₂/tonne verified

**✓** Competitive pricing — 10–20% below Chinese tier-1 mills on base cost for bars and wire

**✓** Growing verification coverage — TUV, SGS, Bureau Veritas now active in India

The downside: **Capacity constraints and fragmented supply.** Only integrated mills and select long-product specialists have verification. Mini-mills and induction furnace operators often lack the capital or expertise.

### The Verdict by Product Category

**Hot-Rolled Coils (HRC, CN 7208)**

**China wins on volume and price.** Chinese HRC is €30–50/tonne cheaper at base cost. But CBAM narrows the gap. For buyers with verified Chinese supply, China remains competitive. For buyers facing default values, Indian HRC from verified mills is now cheaper.

**Bars and Rods (CN 7214)**

**India wins on carbon and total cost.** Verified Indian bar mills at 0.684 tCO₂/tonne vs. Chinese BF-BOF at 2.1+ tCO₂/tonne. On a 300-tonne order, the CBAM difference alone is €900–1,200 in favor of India.

**Wire Rod (CN 7213)**

**Split decision.** Japanese wire rod dominates high-end applications. For standard grades, verified Indian wire rod is now cost-competitive with Chinese supply when CBAM is included.

**Stainless and Alloy Bars**

**India and China are comparable.** Both produce AISI 304, 316, and alloy grades. The deciding factor is verification, not origin. A verified Indian mill at 0.8 tCO₂/tonne beats an unverified Chinese mill at 2.5 tCO₂/tonne every time.

## What to Ask Your Supplier — Regardless of Origin

Before placing any order with an Asian mill, confirm these four items:

**1.** **Verified emissions certificate number.** Who verified? (TUV, SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, DNV). When? Is it current for 2026?

**2.** **Installation-level data.** Is the verification for the specific furnace and rolling mill that will produce your steel? Or is it a corporate average?

**3.** **CN code coverage.** Does the certificate cover your specific product category? A mill verified for HRC may not be verified for bars.

**4.** **CBAM Communication Template (CT) v2.1.1.** Ask for the actual file submitted to the EU registry. The assurance statement alone is not enough.

If your supplier cannot provide all four, you are buying into a **€200/tonne default penalty** — regardless of whether the steel comes from India, China, or anywhere else.
