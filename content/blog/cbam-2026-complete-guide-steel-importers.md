## What Changed in 2026

The **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** entered its definitive phase on **January 1, 2026**. For European steel buyers, this is no longer a reporting exercise. It is a direct cost on every tonne imported from outside the EU.

If you import steel from **India, Japan, China, Vietnam,** or any third country, you now face three obligations:

**1.** Report embedded emissions data for each shipment

**2.** Purchase CBAM certificates to cover the carbon cost of those emissions

**3.** Surrender certificates annually by September 30 each year

Miss any of these, and your shipments face penalties, delays, or refusal at customs.

## What CBAM Actually Costs

The biggest misconception about CBAM is that it is a flat tax. **It is not.**

The actual cost depends on two factors.

### Factor 1: Actual vs. Default Emissions Data

**Actual Emissions (Self-Reported or Audited)**

Source: Mill's own measurement or third-party assurance (e.g., ISO 14064-3)  
Cost Impact: **Lower cost** — you pay for the real carbon footprint  
Note: The EU has not yet established an accreditation system for CBAM verifiers. Third-party assurance statements (e.g., from SGS, TUV, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, DNV) are not legally binding CBAM verifications but may be accepted by customs as supporting evidence.

**Default Values**

Source: EU Commission fallback assumptions  
Cost Impact: **20-30% higher** — worst-case scenario applied

For steel, the EU default value for direct emissions is approximately **2.5 tonnes CO₂ per tonne of steel**. If your mill produces at 1.8 tonnes CO₂ per tonne, using actual data saves you **0.7 tonnes x EUR 80/tonne = EUR 56 per tonne**.

On a 100-tonne shipment, that is **EUR 5,600 in savings** compared to default values.

### Factor 2: The Free Allocation Adjustment

The EU still gives free carbon allowances to domestic steel producers. To level the playing field, CBAM importers receive a corresponding reduction in liability.

In 2026, the **CBAM Factor is 97.5%**. This means EU domestic producers still receive **97.5%** of their free allowances. CBAM importers get a proportional reduction in their liability to match.

This reduction applies **equally** to both actual and default emissions data. The key difference is the starting point:

**Actual data:** Starts from your mill's real emissions (e.g., 1.8 tCO₂/tonne)

**Default values:** Starts from the EU worst-case assumption (2.5 tCO₂/tonne)

Both are then reduced by the same free allocation adjustment. The savings from actual data come from the lower starting emissions, not from the CBAM factor itself.

## The Steel Buyer's CBAM Checklist

Before placing any order with an Asian mill, confirm these five items.

### 1\. Emissions Data Availability

Does the mill provide actual emissions data for your specific product and production route? This can be self-reported or supported by third-party assurance under ISO 14064-3. While the EU has not yet established an accreditation system for CBAM verifiers, assurance from recognized bodies (SGS, TUV, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, DNV) strengthens data reliability and may be accepted by customs as supporting evidence.

### 2\. Melt and Pour Origin

EU anti-circumvention rules require proof of where the steel was first produced. A bill of lading from a trading company is not enough. You need:

\- Continuous casting records

\- Heat numbers linked to chemical analysis

\- Mill test certificates (EN 10204 3.1 or 3.2)

### 3\. CN Code Accuracy

Steel products cover multiple CN chapters. Misclassification means wrong CBAM rates and potential customs penalties. Common codes:

**7208** — Hot-rolled flat products

**7214** — Bars and rods (hot-rolled)

**7217** — Wire of iron or non-alloy steel

**7304** — Tubes, pipes, hollow profiles (seamless)

### 4\. Quota Timing

EU safeguard quotas are quarterly and product-specific. Importing out-of-quota triggers **50% additional duty**. Coordinate shipment timing with quota availability.

### 5\. Certificate Purchase Plan

You must hold at least **50% of your estimated annual CBAM certificate requirement** in your account at each quarter-end. Budget accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to become a CBAM declarant myself?

Only if you import directly. If you buy from a European importer of record (like Kinzoku), they handle CBAM on your behalf.

### What if my supplier cannot provide actual emissions data?

You pay default values — significantly more. We recommend sourcing from mills that have undergone third-party assurance under ISO 14064-3 from SGS, TUV, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, or DNV. While not legally binding CBAM verifications, these assurance statements strengthen data reliability and may be accepted by customs.

### Can I use the same emissions data for multiple shipments?

Yes, if the production process and fuel mix are unchanged. Annual re-verification or re-assurance is standard.

### Does CBAM apply to stainless steel?

Yes. All iron and steel products in CN Chapters 72 and 73 are covered.

### What happens if I do not surrender certificates on time?

Penalties of EUR 100-200 per tonne of CO2, plus potential customs enforcement action.
