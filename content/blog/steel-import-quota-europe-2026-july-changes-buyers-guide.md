European steel safeguard quotas are tightening. Here is what changed, what it means for your supply chain, and how to secure material before the window closes.

## What Changed on July 1, 2026

The EU **steel safeguard measures** were revised effective **July 1, 2026**. For European manufacturers importing steel from outside the EU, this is the most significant quota adjustment since the system began in 2018.

Here is what you need to know in three sentences:

**1.** Quota volumes for most product categories have been **reduced by up to 47%** compared to the previous period.

**2.** Once a quarterly quota is exhausted, imports of that product category face an **additional 50% duty** for the remainder of the quarter.

**3.** The quota is allocated on a **first-come, first-served basis** — there is no reservation system for regular buyers.

This means that if you are not actively monitoring quota consumption and timing your shipments, you are paying significantly more — or missing delivery windows entirely.

## Which Products Are Affected

The safeguard covers **26 product categories** across CN Chapters 72 and 73. The categories most relevant to European manufacturers include:

**Hot-Rolled Flat Products (CN 7208)**

Quota cut: **~45%** | Most consumed category | Exhaustion typically by week 6 of quarter

**Cold-Rolled Flat Products (CN 7209)**

Quota cut: **~40%** | Automotive and appliance demand high

**Bars and Rods (CN 7214)**

Quota cut: **~42%** | Construction and machinery sectors

**Wire Rod (CN 7213)**

Quota cut: **~38%** | Fastener and wire drawing industries

**Seamless Tubes (CN 7304)**

Quota cut: **~35%** | Energy and automotive applications

Each category has its own quarterly allocation. A category that exhausts in week 5 cannot receive more imports at the standard duty rate until the next quarter begins.

## The 50% Out-of-Quota Penalty: A Real Example

Here is what happens when a shipment arrives after the quota is exhausted.

A German manufacturer orders **150 tonnes of hot-rolled coil** from an Indian mill. The standard import duty is **25%**. The shipment is scheduled to arrive in week 8 of the quarter.

However, the hot-rolled quota was exhausted in week 6. The shipment now falls into the **out-of-quota rate**:

**Standard duty:** 25% of customs value

**Out-of-quota surcharge:** +50% of customs value

**Total duty:** 75% of customs value

On a €600/tonne HRC shipment, that is **€450/tonne in duty alone** — compared to €150/tonne if the shipment had landed in week 4. The difference of **€300/tonne × 150 tonnes = €45,000** is a cost that cannot be recovered from the end customer.

This is not theoretical. It is happening now to buyers who did not coordinate quota timing with their suppliers.

## How to Monitor Quota Availability

The European Commission publishes **daily quota consumption data** on the TARIC database. Here is how to use it:

**Step 1:** Identify your product's CN code and corresponding quota category.

**Step 2:** Check the TARIC database for real-time consumption of that category.

**Step 3:** Calculate your shipment's arrival date and confirm quota availability for that quarter.

**Step 4:** If the quota is approaching exhaustion, either accelerate shipment or delay to the next quarter.

The challenge is that this requires **daily monitoring and real-time coordination with your mill and freight forwarder**. Most mid-size buyers do not have the bandwidth.

## The Quota-CBAM Double Hit

Quota exhaustion is not the only risk. Since January 1, 2026, every import also carries a **CBAM carbon cost**. The two systems operate independently:

**Safeguard quotas** control _volume_ — how much can enter at standard duty rates.

**CBAM** controls _carbon cost_ — how much you pay for embedded emissions.

A shipment that lands out-of-quota faces both the **50% surcharge** and the **full CBAM liability**. The combined cost can make Asian steel more expensive than domestic European supply — defeating the entire purpose of importing.

The only way to avoid this is **proactive coordination**: monitor quotas, verify emissions data, and time shipments to land inside both the duty-free window and the CBAM compliance window.
