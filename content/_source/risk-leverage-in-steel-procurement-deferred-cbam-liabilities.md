# Risk Leverage in Steel Procurement: The Reality of Deferred CBAM Liabilities

Why European steel buyers are shifting from standard import frameworks to fixed, fully-inclusive delivered pricing — and what that means for your procurement strategy.

## The Hidden Cost No One Is Talking About

The **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** is no longer just a reporting obligation. Since January 1, 2026, it has become a direct financial liability on every tonne of steel imported into the EU from outside the bloc.

But here is the problem most procurement teams have not fully internalized: **the cost is deferred.**

The steel you import today will not have its CBAM liability settled until **September 30, 2027**. That is more than 18 months of floating, unhedged carbon cost sitting on your balance sheet — with no fixed price, no ceiling, and no guarantee.

For European manufacturers, this creates a fundamental operational paradox. You are signing raw material contracts today and accepting an unknown compliance cost that will not be finalized until long after the steel has been processed, sold, and invoiced to your own customers.

## Why Standard Import Frameworks Are Breaking Down

The inaugural **Global CBAM Summit in Prague** made one thing clear: the market is moving past basic administrative reporting and into a phase defined by long-term financial risk management.

Tier-one European buyers are increasingly rejecting the traditional import model — where the buyer absorbs all regulatory volatility — and instead demanding a **fixed, fully-inclusive delivered price** from their counterpart.

This is not a preference. It is a structural necessity. Here's why:

### Reason 1: The Default Value Trap

The European Commission is shifting away from generic default values toward **mandatory, furnace-level installation metrics**. If your supplier cannot provide verified, installation-specific emissions data, you face retroactive default penalties that can increase your CBAM cost by **20–30%**.

On a 500-tonne shipment, that is a potential swing of **EUR 8,000–12,000** — per delivery — that you cannot price into your customer contracts today.

### Reason 2: Opaque Trading Houses Cannot Adapt

Traditional import brokers and opaque trading houses lack the data infrastructure to provide verified emissions at the furnace level. They rely on default assumptions, which means their pricing is inherently unstable.

European buyers with strict procurement governance cannot accept this. They need:

**✓** Verified emissions data from an EU-recognized body

**✓** Fixed, predictable carbon compliance costs

**✓** A single invoice with no surprise adjustments

### Reason 3: Balance Sheet Risk Is Real

Deferred CBAM liabilities are appearing on EU corporate balance sheets as contingent obligations. Auditors and CFOs are asking hard questions: _What is our actual carbon exposure? Is it hedged? Can our supplier guarantee the number?_

If the answer is no, the procurement decision becomes a board-level risk issue.
