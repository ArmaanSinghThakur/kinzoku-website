## Why Scandinavian Manufacturers Are Turning to Asian Steel

Scandinavia's industrial base — automotive, wind energy, oil & gas, forestry machinery — depends on steel. With Russian supply lines disrupted, domestic mills at capacity, and EU quotas tightening, Asian sourcing is no longer optional. It is strategic.

🇸🇪

### Sweden

Volvo, Scania, SSAB demand high-grade steel. Automotive and construction sectors growing. Liberalerna MEP Karin Karlsbro led EU steel tariff legislation — market is politically engaged and compliance-focused.

🇳🇴

### Norway

Oil & gas infrastructure, shipbuilding, offshore wind. High import prices (avg $4,390/tonne for electrical steel) create margin for competitive Asian sourcing. Premium market, quality-sensitive.

🇩🇰

### Denmark

Wind energy boom drives massive steel demand. Import growth of 2,286% in some categories. Ørsted and Vestas need reliable, compliant supply chains.

🇫🇮

### Finland

Forestry machinery, metals processing, Russian supply replacement needed. Metso Outotec, Valmet, and Wärtsilä require consistent engineering steel.

## The Challenge: Distance + Compliance + Quotas

Sourcing from Asia to Scandinavia introduces three layers of complexity that most trading partners cannot handle:

**1\. Logistics** — Rotterdam to Gothenburg/Oslo/Copenhagen is an additional 2–5 days vs. direct Rotterdam delivery. Winter weather can add delays.

**2\. CBAM + Quotas** — Same rules as continental EU, but fewer local customs brokers understand Asian steel documentation.

**3\. Quality Verification** — Scandinavian buyers have among the strictest quality standards in Europe. EN 10204 3.2 (inspection by buyer's representative) is often required, not 3.1.
