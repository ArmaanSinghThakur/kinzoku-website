### FAQs

| Category | Terminology | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finish | Bright | These are plain, untreated steel nails with no protective coating. They have a shiny look but rust easily, making them suitable only for dry indoor use. |
| Hot Dipped Galvanized | The nails are dipped into molten zinc creating a thick and rugged zinc layer that chemically bonds with the steel, making them ideal for outdoor work. |
| Electro Galvanized | The nails are coated with a thin layer of zinc using an electrical current (electroplating). The coating is smooth, shiny, and thin. EG nails offer minimal rust protection and are best for indoor or temporary applications. |
| Polished | These are clean, smooth steel nails that have a shiny, buffed finish. They drive smoothly into wood without extra residue. |
| Yellow Coated | These feature a yellow passivated zinc plating or a colored anti-corrosive resin coating. The yellow layer provides extra defense against rust and helps identify specific fastener types in industrial applications like pallet making. |
| Shank Type | Smooth | A plain, straight shaft with no ridges or markings. These are standard for general construction, drive in quickly, and are easy to remove, but they offer less holding power. |
| Ring / Annular Threaded | Features a series of stacked, ring-like ridges or beveled notches along the shaft. As you drive the nail, wood fibers lock into the rings to create high friction and prevent the nail from backing out. |
| Screw / Threaded / Spiral | Features a twisted, helical spiral down the shaft that resembles a wood screw. While still driven straight in with a nail gun, the spiral design turns as it enters dense materials, providing maximum pull-out resistance in hardwoods. |
| Point Type | Diamond Point | Standard general-purpose tapered tip. |
| Clinch Point | Bends into a hook upon hitting a metal surface. |
| Chisel Point | Flat, blade-like edge that cuts fibers to reduce splitting. |
| Blunt Point / Blunt Diamond | Flattened or dull tip that punches through fibers instead of splitting dense wood. |
| Pointless / Flat Point | Lacks a sharp tip entirely for specialized low-splitting or automated needs. |
| Long / Needle Point | Extended sharp tip for precision or minimal resistance tasks. |
| Application | Roofing Coil Nails | Short, wide-head nails designed for attaching shingles and roofing materials. |
| Framing Coil Nails | Longer, heavy-duty nails used in structural framing and wood joining. |
| Siding Coil Nails | Designed with smaller heads or rings to reduce visibility and hold siding materials securely. |
| Pallet Coil Nails | Ideal for high-speed pallet and crate manufacturing - these nails are optimized for consistent performance on industrial lines. |
| Drywall and Sheathing Nails | Engineered to prevent material damage while securing panels in place. |
| Application by Industry | Construction and Framing | Used for framing walls, roofing, decking, sheathing, and subfloors. Their strong grip and fast installation make them indispensable in residential and commercial building projects. |
| Pallet and Crate Manufacturing | In high-volume environments, such as pallet factories, coil nails are the standard. Their speed and reliability make them perfect for automated nailing systems. |
| Furniture and Cabinetry | For quick assembly of furniture frames or backs, especially when using soft or engineered woods. |
| Siding and Fencing | Specialized coil nails for siding provide the corrosion resistance and appearance needed for exterior wood paneling or fencing. |
| DIY and Home Renovation | More advanced DIYers use coil nails with portable pneumatic nailers for remodeling, decking, and roofing projects. |
